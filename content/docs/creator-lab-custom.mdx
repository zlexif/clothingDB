title: Your own clothing
description: Add clothing files from your computer to the Creator Lab, put them on the ped and photograph them like the game's own clothing.

The Creator Lab can show clothing that is not in the game, such as your server's
own clothing or a piece you are making. Add its files, and it goes on the real
ped like any other item. Everything else works the same: Only the garment,
backdrops, angles, framing and Capture view.

Your files are read in your browser and never leave your computer.

## What you need

FiveM clothing comes as two kinds of file:

| File | What it is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **.ydd** | The model: the shape of the piece | `jbib_000_u.ydd` |
| **.ytd** | A texture: one colour or design of it | `jbib_diff_000_a_uni.ytd` |

Add the model and as many of its textures as you like. Each texture becomes one
colour you can switch between. They are usually in the resource's `stream`
folder.

<Callout type="warn" title="Escrowed clothing cannot be added">
  Escrowed clothing is encrypted, and only FiveM can open it, on a server that
  owns it. The Creator Lab reads the files in your browser, outside FiveM, so it
  cannot open escrowed files. They are recognised and turned away with a short
  explanation. To get pictures of escrowed clothing, take screenshots in game on
  a green or magenta background and use [Key captures](/docs/key-captures).
</Callout>

## Add a piece

<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### Open the window

    In the Ped studio, press **Add your own clothing** at the top of the left
    column.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Drop the files in

    Drop the .ydd and its .ytd files on the window, drop the whole folder, or
    click to choose them. You can add several pieces at once.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Check the ped and slot

    Each piece shows its name, how many colours were found, and which ped and
    slot it goes on. Both are read from the file names. Change them if they are
    wrong. A slot outlined in amber means the name did not say, so pick it
    yourself.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Add it

    Press **Add and wear it**. The piece goes straight on the ped. If it was made
    for the other ped, the studio switches to that ped first.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Use it

Your pieces are listed under **Your clothing**:

- **Wear** puts a piece back on the ped.
- The box icon on a worn piece photographs **every colour** of it and saves one
  ZIP, laid out as `{ped}/{slot}/{name}/{colour}`.
- The bin icon removes the piece.

In the outfit list, a slot wearing your piece shows its name. The counter next
to it switches between its colours, and **Use game items** goes back to the
game's clothing for that slot.

**Capture view** names the picture after your piece, for example
`clothingDB-jbib_000_u-2.webp` for its third colour.

## How files are matched

The Creator Lab reads FiveM's usual file names:

```txt
mp_m_freemode_01^jbib_000_u.ydd            male, Tops / Jackets, item 0
mp_m_freemode_01^jbib_diff_000_a_uni.ytd   its first colour (a)
mp_m_freemode_01^jbib_diff_000_b_uni.ytd   its second colour (b)
```

- The slot comes from the four letters at the start: `jbib` is Tops / Jackets,
  `lowr` is Legs, `feet` is Shoes, `uppr` is Torsos, `accs` is Undershirts,
  `berd` is Masks, `hair` is Hair, `teef` is Accessories, `task` is Armor,
  `hand` is Bags and `decl` is Decals.
- The ped comes from `mp_m_freemode_01` (male) or `mp_f_freemode_01` (female).
  If the name does not say, the current ped is used.
- Textures go with the model that has the same slot and number. Their letters
  (a, b, c…) set the order of the colours.
- If you add one model and its textures are named differently, all of them go
  with that model.

## What is not supported

| File | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Escrowed (encrypted) files | They only open inside FiveM |
| GTA V Enhanced files | They use a newer format that is not read yet |
| Props: hats, glasses, watches | The Ped studio does not show props yet |
| A model with no texture | It still works, but shows in plain grey |

## Limits

These keep your browser from freezing on a broken or oversized file. Normal
clothing is far below all of them.

| Limit | Size |
| --- | --- |
| One file | 64 MB |
| One file once unpacked | 256 MB |
| One texture | 4096 × 4096 pixels |
| Clothing files read in one go | 200. Add the rest in another go |

Some newer clothing uses a texture format (BC7) that a few browsers, mostly on
phones, cannot display. On a computer it works in every major browser.
